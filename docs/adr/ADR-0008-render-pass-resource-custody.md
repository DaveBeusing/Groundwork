## Problem and evidence

The existing D3D12 backend records an explicit finite rendering sequence with a single render owner, established resource-state transitions, bounded admission and fence-based retirement. GpuPipeline.cs, GpuShadows.cs, GpuRetirement.cs, D3D12RenderOwner.cs and WindowsSwapChain.cs show these mechanisms. Groundwork already renders terrain, shadows, sky and props; it is not a blank renderer.

Adding material textures, HDR, multiple lights and postprocess targets increases the number of cross-pass resource dependencies. An unstructured extension would make ownership and hazard review expensive. No measurement currently proves that a full render graph is faster or necessary.

## Proposed decision


1. **Keep execution and authority with the existing renderer.** Validate declarations for the current fixed-order D3D12 direct queue. The exclusive render owner records and submits work. Core remains the sole authority for simulation, terrain and collision publication. Render planning, missing assets and redraw are observer work outside the fixed-tick callbacks.
2. **Declare dependencies and bounded work.** Specify stable pass IDs, ordered inputs and outputs, read/write intents, imported or produced resources, initialized subresource ranges, required states, clear/load/store behavior, extent and terminal use. An import declares an expected identity and state. In-frame reads and conflicting accesses require an ordered dependency and initialized data. Reject invalid states, aliasing, use of unwritten or discarded contents, cycles and undeclared dependencies. Preserve explicit order where required and use stable IDs to break otherwise equivalent ordering ties. Cap passes, edges, declarations, subresource ranges, and scratch before validation. Reuse immutable validated topology where possible and recheck live bindings for each recording. No per-frame blocking I/O, shader compilation or optional CPU/GPU waits.
3. **Separate portable declarations from live custody.** Portable descriptions carry format, extent, usage, admission class and opaque typed generation-aware resource identities. They contain no D3D12 handles or fence authority. RPA-02 establishes the render-owner handoff used by later uploads. Before recording, resolve expected resources and states against the actual queue/device/renderer epoch and the state left by prior ordered submissions. A mismatch rejects the plan before execution. Use the owner's existing native transitions. An aborted recording leaves trusted state and published bindings intact and preserves existing cache invalidation. On successful enqueue and signal, the owner advances the trusted queue-ordered state. A later sample requires initialized mips and a copy and transition ordered ahead of that use. Installation follows ADR-0010's upload retirement rule.
4. **Cover current execution variants.** Describe color/depth clear, conditional shadow cascade writes or cache imports, optional sky, terrain, props and applicable follow-on scene, then readback or the swap-chain Present transition. Model a cached cascade as an initialized shader-readable import with a matching key and generation. On a cache miss use the existing redraw or disabled branch. Publish cache validity only with eligible submission. Retain the current sequencing and native barriers. Any full render graph requires a separate decision supported by measured benefits.
5. **Retain submission custody.** Track native allocations, staging, backbuffers and the exact descriptor slot/table/heap versions through their applicable copy, draw or presentation fence on the existing queue. The existing draw signal and separate post-Present signal retain their distinct roles. Use one backend-owned shader-visible binding for shadow and material SRVs consumed by the same draw. Cap slots and heap versions and reserve fallback capacity. Replace through a separately allocated version or reuse a retired slot. A failure after possible execution with no assigned signal closes admission and retains all touched objects and credits under the existing quarantine until proven retirement or confirmed removal. A fence sentinel alone establishes neither. Preserve stop, resize, occlusion, DRED and cleanup ownership.
6. **Keep the currently authorized view.** At the render-owner frame boundary, capture the authorized world, renderer epoch, view, terrain/collision publication and origin, plus one immutable material palette and binding revision for all consumers. Preserve the existing producer-side WP11 first-content registration and its transfer of the retained reader before the paired frame reaches the owner. The owner captures appearance after that registration, before recording and final submission authorization. For later camera frames, capture appearance before authorizing the exact camera sequence against the acknowledged registration. Keep the WP11 revocation and authorization cut immediately before native submission. Reject invalid content without a content Present receipt. Reuse prior content only if it remains authorized for the same current view and its plan and bindings are valid. Honor WP11 neutral mask, hold and recovery after revocation, cutover, resize or rebase. A valid neutral frame can still carry its existing mask receipt. Prior in-flight work settles through existing custody. Only a successful normal Present followed by retirement and current-view revalidation can produce the existing content receipt for Core's BeginTick verification. A stale or revoked frame cannot regain authority. If the first content is already registered and rejected before native execution, retain at most the one exact paired registration, sequence and its reader under existing credit. Retry only on an eligible owner wake, after revalidating the still-current registration and a corrected plan or binding, with a bounded attempt/time limit. If revoked, invalid without an admissible correction or out of retry budget, settle the unexecuted registration through existing reader/native custody and request the WP11 Recovery/Retired path for the same committed root. Core alone may authorize a fresh view/permit at BeginTick. Keep the mask and action hold, issue no content receipt, and do not substitute a camera or appearance redraw for the first content. Preserve unrelated tick progress and existing stop and incomplete-cleanup semantics. See [ADR-0009](ADR-0009-pbr-material-model.md) for binding installation.
7. **Protect frame progress and report faults.** RPA-02 reuses the owner's existing yield points. Optional validation and redraw must be bounded and cannot hold protected terrain readiness, masking or recovery behind asset availability. Reuse the bounded diagnostics path and #88 trace format. Report pass and barrier counts, state and fence occupancy, correlated protected-ready-to-normal-Present, Present-to-retired-receipt, retired-receipt-to-BeginTick and end-to-end ready-to-BeginTick latency for the same frame/view, and simulation tick wake/debt/steps under pressure. Record terminal renderer faults and incomplete cleanup via the existing bounded owner/runtime safety path independently of optional diagnostics.

## Alternatives

- **Continue only ad hoc call ordering:** minimal immediate code; rejected as the default expansion strategy because pass state and ownership would be under-specified.
- **Install a full Render Graph immediately:** could simplify transient allocation and automatic barriers; deferred because it would modify working ownership and render scheduling before a workload or benchmark justifies it.
- **A minimal declaration/validation layer with ordered execution (selected proposal):** incremental, testable, and compatible with the existing retirement model; costs some metadata and validation code.

## First implementation slices

- RPA-01: portable pass/resource descriptors and a pure graph/hazard validator. No native execution changes.
- RPA-02: adapt the full current sequence and establish the native imported-state and custody handoff before TEX-02. Preserve existing ordering, transitions, fallback and output.
- RPA-03: optional resource-lifetime diagnostics and a narrowly scoped transient-target prototype if HDR later needs it. Do not merge speculative aliasing without measurements.

## Required evidence / acceptance


- RPA-01 portable fixtures cover deterministic ordering, imported initialized inputs, branches, clear-only and postprocessing, conflicting access, cycles, stale generations, invalid state and extent, invalid subresource ranges and rejected over-cap plans.
- RPA-02 compares fixed-seed WARP pixels and pass/barrier counts for clear and sky-only, shadow off, shadow cache hit/miss, terrain with props, readback and Present, edit-first-Present and near/far handoff. Preserve unchanged pixels and the existing WP15 shadow work. Reject wrong view, origin or epoch and invalid plans before or after frame registration but before execution. Inject rejection after the neutral mask and a terrain commit. Verify no revoked draw or false content receipt, including on resize and recovery. Inject stale binding arrival and a pre-execution rejection of the registered first content. Verify retry of only the still-current registration, and separately test a revoked or exhausted registration followed by WP11 re-admission. The matching newly presented terrain must produce exactly one eligible BeginTick release. Hold the assigned post-Present fence independently and verify no early content receipt or action release, with unrelated ticks continuing.
- Verify a recording abort and a live imported-state mismatch, including a mip left in copy-destination state. Exercise post-execution Signal failure, delayed fence, synthetic removal sentinel, confirmed removal, occlusion, resize and stop. Require no early reuse and zero live GPU child objects after successful cleanup on the applicable WARP and NVIDIA Debug Layer/DRED routes.
- Pair Windows Release reference runs. Record CPU validation and record latency, allocation, GPU pass time, frame p50/p95/p99, memory, draws, barriers and protected publication timing under concurrent pressure. Retain WP11, WP15 and WP16 limits and zero extra brick requests for camera or material changes. Use #88 for sizing and #89 for adversarial qualification.

## Consequences / rollback

Positive: explicit contracts for postprocessing and future material passes; easier correctness and profiling. Negative: additional metadata and potential validation cost; an initial ordered approach does not optimize pass overlap. Rollback is confined to new pass description/adapter code; no forced conversion of existing worlds, saves or Game APIs.
